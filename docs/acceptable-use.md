# Acceptable-use terms

> [!CAUTION]
> **NO MALICIOUS OR UNAUTHORIZED USE**
>
> **PTP is absolutely not intended to be used for malicious behavior. Such use is unacceptable and expressly prohibited by these project acceptable-use terms.**
>
> Use PTP only on systems you own or for which you have explicit authorization to install and operate a file-access endpoint and perform the intended file operations. Access to a website, a WordPress account, credentials, or an upload feature is not blanket authorization.

## Prohibited behavior

You must not use PTP to:

- Access, read, copy, modify, or delete files outside the owner's authorized scope.
- Steal or expose private data, credentials, customer information, or other confidential material.
- Upload malware, plant a backdoor, establish hidden access, or conceal unauthorized changes.
- Deface, sabotage, disrupt, or intentionally damage a website, service, or its data.
- Circumvent access restrictions imposed by the owner or hosting provider without authorization.
- Keep or regain access after a maintenance engagement ends or permission is withdrawn.
- Assist another person in carrying out any of these activities.

Calling an action “testing,” “debugging,” or “maintenance” does not make it authorized. Malicious behavior is outside the project's intended use and violates these acceptable-use terms.

## What authorized use requires

Before deploying PTP, agree with the owner on the site, directories, operations, and duration of access. Permission to edit content does not automatically include permission to deploy an endpoint that can write executable code.

Respect the owner's instructions, hosting-provider rules, and applicable law. Keep credentials private, avoid accessing unrelated data, and remove or disable temporary access when the work ends. If permission is unclear, stop and resolve it with the owner before proceeding.

An owner declining to provide FTP access is not, by itself, permission to install an alternative. PTP exists to solve an approved maintenance problem, not to override that decision.
